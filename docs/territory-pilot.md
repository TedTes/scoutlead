# Territory Pilot

## Initial customer

The first pilot targets Canadian commercial insurance brokers that repeatedly sell to
independent HVAC, roofing, and plumbing contractors in the Toronto/GTA market.

This is deliberately narrower than the eventual market. The pilot must prove that a maintained
local territory produces qualified sales conversations before ScoutLead expands to other sellers
or local-business niches.

## Product contract

- **Offer:** what a workspace sells, the customer it serves, observable fit signals, and explicit
  exclusions.
- **Territory:** one confirmed offer, niche, and market combination.
- **Delivery:** a dated batch of prospects produced for a territory by one discovery run.
- **Qualified contact:** a business/contact pair that passes offer-fit policy and has evidence,
  provenance, and an allowed outreach policy.
- **Outcome:** an immutable event describing fit feedback, contact activity, or a commercial
  result.
- **New prospect:** a business eligible for this workspace and offer under the recontact policy.
  It does not mean the business itself was newly established.

## Pilot operation

- 3-5 pilot workspaces.
- One active territory per workspace.
- 15-25 contacts per manually triggered weekly delivery.
- Four consecutive delivery weeks before scheduler work begins.
- No territory email is sent without sender identity, an outreach basis, and unsubscribe support.

## Measures

- At least 70% of delivered contacts are accepted as worth contacting.
- At least 80% have a valid, usable contact channel.
- Fewer than 5% are duplicates, closed businesses, or outside the confirmed territory.
- At least 60% of accepted contacts are contacted.
- At least three pilot customers request a fourth weekly delivery.
- At least two pilot customers agree to pay to continue.

Automatic `no_response` events are reported separately and do not count as manually observed
outcome coverage.

## Decision gate

If fit acceptance, data quality, or week-four retention misses the thresholds, improve sourcing
and qualification before adding scheduling or outcome-weighted ranking. Increasing list volume is
not a substitute for meeting the quality thresholds.
